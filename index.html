      How Starcast works

      Starcast blends the hourly weather forecast, the sun and moon's computed positions, and your local light pollution into a single 0–1 stargazing score for every hour of the next two weeks. Pick a day, scrub to an hour, and every tile updates to that exact moment. Swipe left or right on the tile grid to change days; the dot under each day tab shows how good that night gets. Tap the verdict banner to see exactly how the score was built, metric by metric.

      The Tonight's Sky card tracks the moon phase and upcoming new/full moons, the Milky Way core's peak altitude, when each visible planet rises and peaks, active meteor showers (with a warning when moonlight will wash them out), the aurora outlook when it matters for your latitude, and whether the major weather models actually agree about tonight's clouds. Transparency estimates use measured aerosol optical depth, and seeing estimates use measured jet-stream winds, whenever those are available. The 📅 button on the best-window pill saves that window to your calendar.

      The eclipse button in the header switches to red night mode — the entire app drops to deep red on black to preserve your eyes' dark adaptation at the telescope (in red mode, brighter red = better conditions). A color-blind-friendly palette is available under Settings → Appearance, and Settings can save multiple locations for quick switching.

      Tip: add Starcast to your home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen) for a full-screen app that opens instantly and keeps working offline with your last-fetched forecast — handy at dark-sky sites with no signal.

      The verdict & tiles

      Verdict banner
      A weighted blend of every metric below (cloud cover counts most at 30%, darkness 20%, precipitation 12%, moon 10%, and so on). Score ≥ 0.66 is "Good To Stargaze", 0.33–0.66 is "Marginal Stargazing", below 0.33 is "Not Good To Stargaze". Hard caps: daylight caps the score at 0.25, ≥ 90% cloud caps it at 0.20, and ≥ 70% precipitation probability caps it at 0.25 — it is never "Good" while the sun is up or the sky is socked in.
      Weather
      The forecast condition icon. Clear skies score 1.0 (green), partly cloudy 0.6 (olive), overcast 0.2 (red), and any fog, drizzle, rain, or snow scores 0.0 (red).
      Sun Times
      Sunrise and sunset for the selected day, straight from Open-Meteo. Informational only — this tile is always olive and never affects the score.
      Visibility
      Surface visibility. 10 miles or more scores 1.0, falling linearly to 0.0 at 2 miles — green above ≈ 7.3 mi, red below ≈ 4.6 mi.
      Wind Chill
      Feels-like temperature, purely a comfort metric. 32–75 °F scores a full 1.0; it falls linearly to 0.0 at 0 °F on the cold side and 100 °F on the hot side.
      Wind
      Wind steadies both you and your telescope. ≤ 5 mph scores 1.0, ≥ 25 mph scores 0.0 — green below ≈ 12 mph, red above ≈ 18 mph.
      Precip Prob
      Chance of precipitation. 0% scores 1.0, ≥ 60% scores 0.0 — green below ≈ 20%, red above ≈ 40%. At ≥ 70% the overall verdict is capped at "Not Good".
      Cloud Cover
      Total cloud cover, the heaviest-weighted metric. 0% scores 1.0, ≥ 80% scores 0.0 — green below ≈ 27%, red above ≈ 53%. The sub-line breaks out Low/Mid/High cloud layers.
      Seeing
      Atmospheric steadiness from 7Timer's ASTRO model, remapped to 0–1 (higher is better): green ≥ 0.66, red < 0.33. A tiny "est." marks hours beyond 7Timer's ~72-hour range, where Starcast estimates it from cloud and wind instead.
      Transparency
      How crisp and dark the sky itself is (haze, moisture, high cloud), also from 7Timer on the same 0–1 scale and thresholds, with the same "est." fallback based on humidity and high cloud.
      Moon
      Illuminated fraction of the moon, computed on-device. If the moon is below the horizon at the selected hour it scores a perfect 1.0 no matter the phase; while it's up, the score is 1 − illumination — green ≤ 34% illuminated, red ≥ 67%.
      Planets
      Which bright planets (Me V Ma J S) are more than 5° above your horizon at the selected hour, from on-device orbital mechanics. Green when 2+ are up, olive for 1, red for none. Doesn't affect the overall score.
      Light Pollution
      Your Bortle class, measured automatically from David Lorenz's World Atlas 2024 zenith-brightness data for your exact coordinates (an estimate — Bortle is a whole-sky visual scale). It updates whenever your location changes. Bortle ≤ 2 scores 1.0, ≥ 8 scores 0.0 — green up to Bortle 4, olive 5–6, red 7+.

      Forecast grid & charts

      The Forecast tab shows a Clear Outside-style hour-by-hour grid for the next 7 days: every row is a metric, every column an hour, every cell colored by the same green/olive/red thresholds listed above. Day headers show sun and moon rise/set times and the astronomical-darkness window (sun below −18°). The Sky row is the overall verdict per hour. On Charts, touch or hover any chart for exact values, switch between 24-hour, 3-day, and 7-day ranges, and read the gold-tinted columns as daylight.
